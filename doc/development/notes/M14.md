**Status:** ✅ done 2026-10-24, on branch `develop`, with **one acceptance target open**: on `onion_shock`, MARKET_SHIFT

## What was built

`tools/simulator/` is a package named `simulator`, put on `PYTHONPATH` by `make sim`/`make sim-all` and by pytest.
It uses `spm` as a library and is linted and type-checked with the backend.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `scenario.py` | Pydantic schema for `scenarios/*.yaml` (the plan's format, plus `targets`, `product_weights`, event `time`, a `spammer` reporter kind) |
| `generator.py` | Ground-truth market + reporters → time-ordered `SimReport`s with `truth ∈ {normal, bad_unit, malicious, shop_outlier, market_shift}` |
| `runner.py` | `inprocess` (default) and `http` replay; collects outcomes, assessments, anomalies and end-of-day band snapshots |
| `maintenance.py` | Nightly stand-in for M17's maintenance (expire PENDING, auto-resolve anomalies after 7 d) |
| `evaluate.py` | Joins predictions with the truth; writes `out/<scenario>-<timestamp>.md` and `.json` |
| `__main__.py` | `python -m simulator [SCENARIO…] [--all] [--mode inprocess\|http] [--days N]` |

### The market model

- **The true price** at a shop at time t: `market(t) × shock(t) × (1 + premium) × gouging(t)`.
  - `market`: a geometric random walk, one step per day, interpolated in log space.
  - `shock`: ramps linearly to its multiplier, then stays.
  - `gouging`: a step for one shop.
- **The fair area price** (what band p50 should track) is the median over shops, without gouging.
- **Days are area-local calendar days.** Events have a local `time`: a market shock starts at 00:00 (wholesale prices
  move overnight), gouging at 08:00 (the shop opens), and the spam burst at 10:00.
- **Reports** are made during shopping hours (08:00–22:00 local) and submitted 1–20 minutes after being observed.
- **Seeding:** each scenario seeds separate random streams for the market and for the crowd, so a seed always gives
  the same run.

### Reporters

| Kind | Behaviour | Truth |
|---|---|---|
| `honest` | true shop price × lognormal noise; buys 1 kg, 500 g or 2 kg (1 dozen, 1 hali or 1 tray for eggs) | `shop_outlier` while gouging; `market_shift` from a shock's start to its ramp + 24 h, while the move is ≥ 5%; otherwise `normal` |
| `sloppy` | honest, but `unit_error_rate` of reports carry a slip: `half` (×0.5), `double` (×2), `magnitude` ("1 g" for 1 kg, or a per-piece egg price entered per dozen) | `bad_unit` |
| `malicious` | +`inflate` at `target_shop`, no GPS | `malicious` |
| `spammer` | one burst of `reports_per_hour` at one shop | `malicious` |

GPS: `gps_rate` of reports have a fix within its accuracy (uniform over `accuracy_m`) of the shop.

### Replay

**In-process** builds the real services:
- `FixedClock` and `MemoryBus`;
- Postgres: the `spm_sim` database, recreated and migrated for each scenario;
- Redis: DB 14, flushed.

For each report it sets the clock to the submission time and calls
`ReportService.submit(…, allow_backfill=True, enforce_rate_limits=True)`. It then drains the bus, so the real scoring
consumer runs.

At each local midnight it runs the maintenance stand-in, then snapshots every product's stored band.

**HTTP mode** posts to `/v1/admin/reports/backfill`, with the timeline shifted to end now. It waits for the live
worker, then reads results from the stack's database. Scoring there runs on the wall clock, so it is a smoke test,
not a source of numbers.

### Report contents

- **Confusion matrix:** truth × {the six classifications, OPEN, REJECTED, DUPLICATE, UNSCORED}.
- **Per-class precision and recall.**
- **True events:** detected or not, time to detect, and alerts (expected kind / all).
- **False alarms,** each with a cause: `bad_data` (built on malicious or bad-unit reports) or `noise`.
- **False SHOP_OUTLIER per product per 30 days.**
- **Band accuracy:** MAPE against the fair price after a warm-up, and band coverage.
- **Freshness per product:** the median band `mean_age_h`, and the median age of the newest report.
- **Guardrail rejections by code.**
- **Targets:** pass or fail.

## Open target: MARKET_SHIFT within 24 h

On the scenario's 4%/day onion random walk, each shop's 30-day baseline has a MAD of 4–6. The shift test is
`|median(last 24 h) − baseline median| / (1.4826 × MAD) > 3`, so a shop must move about 20–29% before it counts, and
three shops must. A 40% shock ramping over 24 h gets there at 2 of 5 shops by the first evening (z 4.1 and 4.3; the
third at 2.0), and at all of them the next morning. Measured:
- **The full scenario,** at 1.9 onion reports per shop per day: 41 h in the final run (35 h with UTC-aligned days).
- **The test version,** at 3 per shop per day: ~42–44 h.

The baseline MAD measures how far the *level* wandered over 30 days (drift), not day-to-day noise. A shorter
baseline (7 d) barely helps (41.8 h). Lowering the z threshold or the floor brings the false alarms back.

**Proposal (needs your decision; it changes M11's classifier):** scale the shift test by the volatility of daily
returns, which `stats.daily_log_returns` and `half_life_from_volatility` already compute, instead of the MAD of
levels. For example: a shop counts when `|ln(recent median / last-day median)| > k × σ_daily × √(window days)`. Until
then, `test_onion_shock_detected_within_24h_once` is a strict `xfail` (it fails loudly once it starts passing), and
`test_onion_shock_detected_by_the_next_day_once` guards ≤ 48 h with exactly one MARKET_SHIFT anomaly.

## Known limitations

- **Replay speed:** ~19–22 reports/s in-process, so `onion_shock` (1,282 reports) takes 68 s. The plan's target
  (45 days × ~50 reports/day, ~2,250 reports, in < 60 s) needs ~37/s.
  - Each report costs ~30 SQL round trips (submit, then scoring, then outbox), at ~1 ms each through the Colima VM.
  - A native Linux Docker host (CI) should be ~2–3× faster.
  - Cutting round trips would mean changing M13's scoring service. It's well inside its own 150 ms budget, so I left it.
- **The e2e target tests add ~85 s** to `make test-int` (now 128 s).
- **Coincident slips look real.** Two independent sloppy reporters making the same slip at the same shop within the
  48 h window corroborate each other and open a SHOP_OUTLIER. That is indistinguishable from a real deal.
- **Colluding malicious reporters** (two inflating shop-a) can likewise confirm each other. The reports label those
  false alarms `bad_data`.
- **Random-walk moves look like market shifts.** In volatile markets, slow random-walk moves are sometimes classified
  MARKET_SHIFT. The truth only knows the scripted events, so these count as false alarms.
- **`checks:` is parsed but not replayed yet** (M15 adds `/check`).
- **HTTP mode can't measure detection time or band accuracy**, because the live stack scores on the wall clock. With
  the backfill endpoint it also never rate-limits.
